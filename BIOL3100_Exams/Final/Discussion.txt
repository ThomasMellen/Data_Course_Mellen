This project examines allometry in the rhinoceros beetle Trypoxylus dichotomus by analyzing how horn length scales with body size across different sample locations. Allometry describes the relationship between the size of a trait and overall body size, and is commonly analyzed using log-transformed data to linearize relationships between traits.  In this analysis, thorax width was used as the measure for body size and compared to horn length. The slopes of the fitted lines represent the allometric scaling relationship, where steeper slopes indicate a larger proportion of horn length to thorax size. Comparing slopes across populations gives insight into how horn length scales with size across different geographic regions, influencing the evolution of these exaggerated structures.
